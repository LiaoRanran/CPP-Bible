The Problem

When you benchmark static analysis tools, you assume the measurement itself is stable. It isn't.

We built Queyi, a framework that audits the evaluator — the measurement pipeline that produces your benchmark scores. We found that switching measurement configurations (OS, compiler flags, accounting rules) can move detection rates by 35+ percentage points without changing a single line of code in the target program.

Silent failure accounting: When a detection asset isn't run, naive accounting treats it as "no bug found" instead of "not measured." Unknown detections stay at 0.00pp — making the measurement look more stable than it is.

What We Found

1. Environmental drift is real and large. On 566 samples, switching from WSL to native Windows dropped joint-asset detection from 60.07% to 24.74% (McNemar p = 1.24e-60).

2. Selection strategy gains are mostly budget effects. The reported +24pp advantage of asset selection over random baselines decomposes into +7.3pp budget tier, −5.1pp pool composition, and only a small residual. Equal-marginal substitution experiments confirm linker effects are near-zero once slot geometry is controlled.

3. Blind spots are structural. 38.4% of defect types have >50% no-capture rates across all 8 assets. This isn't a recall problem — it's a coverage problem.
