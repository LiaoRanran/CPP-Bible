The Problem

You think you're measuring code quality.
You're actually measuring your measurement pipeline.

When a detector isn't run on a config — because it crashed, or the platform didn't support it — naive accounting silently records that as "no bug found" instead of "not measured." The score looks rock-solid. It isn't.

Three Findings

What the audit turned up

🔴

Environmental Drift

60% → 25%

Same code, two machines, ten seconds apart. Joint-asset detection collapses by 35pp the moment the measurement environment changes (McNemar p = 1.24e-60).

🟡

The Selection Gain Is Fake

+24pp

The headline "+24pp advantage" decomposes into +7pp budget tier, −5pp pool composition, and little else. Control the slot geometry and the magic mostly disappears.

🔵

Structural Blind Spots

38.4%

Over a third of defect samples are invisible to every one of the 8 detectors. This isn't low recall — it's a coverage hole in the whole family.

Method

We don't trust the evaluator. We audit it.

01

Freeze the Matrix

Lock every detector verdict into an immutable, append-only ledger. No re-runs, no drift-by-hand.

02

Replay Across Environments

Re-run the same frozen matrix on WSL, native Windows, and cross-toolchains to expose silent gaps.

03

Decompose by Accounting

Split each score into budget, pool, and residual — so fake gains can't hide inside the average.

04

Report What's Silent

Surface the "unknown" that naive benchmarks bury. The honest number is the useful one.
