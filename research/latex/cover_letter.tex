To the NeurIPS Evaluations \& Datasets Chairs and Reviewers,

\bigskip
\noindent
We submit \textbf{``Evolving Verifiers: Failure-Driven Evidence Acquisition with Auditable
Provenance''} for consideration in the \textbf{Evaluations \& Datasets (E\&D)} track, renamed from
Datasets \& Benchmarks in 2026 and (since 2026) \textbf{typically double-blind}; we target the 2027
edition. \emph{Throughout, ``verifier'' is used in the evidence-acquisition sense}: a
\texttt{pass} verdict reports that no acquired evidence contradicts the assertion within our
capability boundary, \textbf{not} that the assertion is semantically true.

\paragraph{Template note.}
The 2027 CFP is not yet released. This draft uses the official \texttt{neurips\_2025} style as a
\emph{placeholder} (the style file is third-party and unmodified); it will migrate to the 2027
E\&D style on release. Croissant and Responsible-AI metadata are registered as pre-submission TODOs
because the 2027 metadata specification is not yet fixed.

\paragraph{Why this fits E\&D.}
Our contribution is an \emph{evaluation methodology} with a fully reproducible artifact and an
explicit \emph{critical / negative} analysis---the three things E\&D asks for. We do not claim a new
model or a new benchmark. We claim a way to make the question ``how well can we verify
LLM-generated technical knowledge?'' an \emph{externally checkable} one.

\paragraph{Core contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Formalized failure-driven verifier evolution.} The verifier's
        evidence-acquisition capability $\mathcal{C}$ evolves under an operator driven
        \emph{only} by registered failures, with three checkable properties and a
        \emph{falsification protocol}: six pre-registered ablations (A0--A5) that can
        \emph{reject} the mechanism. Auditability: Merkle roots over \textbf{5} controlled
        directories, a \textbf{452}-event authority ledger, and a \emph{kernel-independent}
        reconciler (current state; historical pinning partial).
  \item \textbf{A large, caliber-audited empirical asset.} \textbf{1147} samples over
        \textbf{34} defect types (normalized closed vocabulary), in \textbf{7} expansion
        batches (expA--G); a 7-dimension audit of all 1147 samples found and repaired
        \textbf{363} cross-source label inconsistencies. Provenance is
        three-valued: \textbf{968} \texttt{self-authored}, \textbf{74}
        \texttt{source-derived-reconstruction} (single-file teaching reconstructions of real
        CVEs/GitHub issues, \textbf{not} original production code), and \textbf{0}
        \texttt{original-external-artifact}. Rates carry a four-state caliber
        (\texttt{unknown} first-class): the denominator alone moves the corpus rate by
        \textbf{9.9pp}.
  \item \textbf{A full-scale falsification experiment (A5), formalized as submodular
        maximization, with robustness measured.}
        \textbf{1137} samples $\times$ \textbf{8} assets $=$ \textbf{9096} real
        \texttt{detect} calls (derivation 571 / evaluation 566, $k{=}4$): FD \textbf{54.6\%}
        vs.\ Random \textbf{30.6\%} ($\Delta$ \textbf{+24.03pp}, $p{=}2.3\times10^{-41}$)
        and vs.\ Static \textbf{24.7\%} (\textbf{+29.9pp}). Asset selection is formalized as
        \textbf{monotone submodular maximization}: the greedy choice attains the optimum here
        (ratio 1.0, exhaustive check), the \textbf{adaptive ceiling is only +2.3pp}, and the
        selection is stable under 5\% detector noise ($\le0.11$pp drop). The pre-registered
        parallel analysis collapses to $\Delta$ \textbf{0.0pp}, so we register it as
        \emph{direction-only} (\texttt{uninterpretable}) with the \emph{selection} effect at
        $\approx$\,\textbf{+7--12pp}; a \emph{clone-family re-split} leaves the endpoint
        unchanged (\textbf{+23.0--+26.7pp}), excluding template leakage, while a
        \emph{family-level bootstrap} gives an \textbf{effective} $n\approx133$--$140$.
  \item \textbf{A capability-boundary map of the instrument.} On the \textbf{1147 $\times$ 8}
        matrix, sample-level detection is \textbf{61.6\%} and the blind spot \textbf{38.4\%};
        \textbf{13 of 34} types exceed 50\% blind (deadlock 84.6\%, endianness 89.3\%; family
        gradient memory 15.7\% $\to$ link/ODR 67.7\%). This is an \emph{instrument-boundary}
        statistic measured on our corpus with our 8-asset pool --- it does \textbf{not} claim
        that 38.4\% of real-world C++ defects are undetectable --- and it scopes every
        headline number to the \emph{visible} region.
  \item \textbf{Open artifact and reproducible pipeline.} Apache-2.0, DCO-signed; a fail-loud
        \texttt{docker/paper/run\_all.sh} and \texttt{REPRODUCE.md}; one-command
        recomputation. (Croissant and Responsible-AI metadata are registered as
        pre-submission TODOs, since the 2027 metadata spec is not yet released.)
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Relation to prior work.}
Benchmark-evolution lines (Benchmark Self-Evolving, ArenaBencher, LiveBench) evolve the
\emph{items}; mutation-guided testing (Meta ACH, MUTGEN) evolves \emph{tests}; formal verification
(seL4, CompCert) provides \emph{proofs}; and MiniCheck evolves \emph{judge weights}.
\textbf{None evolves the measuring stick itself while
proving the change is externally measurable and self-falsifiable.} That is our position.

\paragraph{Headline result.}
On the \emph{same} samples, failure-driven verification reaches \textbf{82.9\%} blind-holdout recall
(34/41, CP95 [67.9, 92.8]) and \textbf{62.5\%} external-corpus recall (40/64, CP95 [49.5, 74.3]),
versus a \emph{static caliber arm} at \textbf{2.4\%} and \textbf{17.2\%} ($\Delta$
\textbf{+80.5pp} [+68.4, +92.6] / \textbf{+45.3pp} [+33.1, +57.5]; exact McNemar
$p=2.3\times10^{-10}$ / $3.7\times10^{-9}$; Cohen's $h\ge0.97$) and a \emph{random proxy arm}
at \textbf{9.8\%} and \textbf{21.9\%} ($\Delta$ \textbf{+73.2pp} [+59.6, +86.7] /
\textbf{+40.6pp} [+28.6, +52.7]; exact McNemar $p=1.9\times10^{-9}$ / $3.0\times10^{-8}$;
Cohen's $h\ge0.85$). The budget-matched control A5 now
runs at full scale (1137 samples; $n{=}566$ held out): FD \textbf{54.6\%} vs.\ Random \textbf{30.6\%}
(\textbf{+24.03}pp, $p{=}2.3\times10^{-41}$) and vs.\ Static \textbf{24.7\%} (\textbf{+29.9}pp),
with the Static contrast surviving the pre-registered parallel analysis (\textbf{+30.6}pp) while
the Random contrast collapses to \textbf{0.0}pp once degenerate assets are excluded. The Random
contrast is \textbf{+11.31}pp at $k{=}1$ on the degenerate-free pool ($p{=}6.0\times10^{-8}$), so
the isolated selection effect is $\approx$\,\textbf{+7--12}pp, and a \textbf{clone-family re-split}
leaves the endpoint unchanged (\textbf{+23.0--+26.7}pp) while a family-level bootstrap gives an
\textbf{effective} $n\approx133$--$140$. We also publish
a capability-boundary map of the instrument itself (sample-level detection \textbf{61.6\%}; \textbf{38.4\%} of 1147 samples are blind spots;
\textbf{13 of 34} normalized types exceed 50\% blind). This blind-spot ratio is an
\emph{instrument-boundary} statistic measured on our corpus with our 8-asset pool: it does
\textbf{not} imply that 38.4\% of real-world C++ defects are undetectable. Provenance in the corpus
is three-valued: 968 \texttt{self-authored}, 74 \texttt{source-derived-reconstruction} (single-file
teaching reconstructions of real CVEs/issues, \textbf{not} original production code), and 0
\texttt{original-external-artifact}. A \textbf{110-defect real-CVE benchmark} (all sample CVEs
verified online via the NVD API, raw responses frozen) reads \textbf{59.09\%} OR detection ---
statistically indistinguishable from the synthetic corpus ($p{=}0.60$) --- with \textbf{93.5\%}
cross-toolchain agreement (clang 18 vs.\ g++ 13.3). The e-process layer is \emph{exploratory}
(priors and alternative grids not pre-registered) and supports no confirmatory claim.

\paragraph{Honest limitations (stated up front).}
The two headline rates depend on a specific WSL sanitizer environment and \textbf{fail silently} when
it is absent (corpus 35.0\% $\to$ 10.0\%) --- the reproducibility script now checks for this and
aborts; the static arm is a \textbf{caliber re-binning} (not a re-run) and the random arm is an
\textbf{instrument-level proxy} (not the paper's budget-matched B3, whose interface is currently
blocked in a split repository); the effective sample size after clone-aware re-splitting is only $n\approx133$--$140$
(design effect $\approx4$), and single-cell verdicts carry $\approx$\,\textbf{5\%}
run-to-run instability (676f self-verified); A5 isolates \emph{pool composition}
(``do not fund assets that cannot inform''), not a smarter ranking, so we claim \textbf{auditability of the mechanism} plus
\textbf{a significant same-sample advantage over the static/proxy arms} --- \textbf{not} superiority
over a true static detector or the real B3. We include this analysis as part of the contribution
rather than hiding it.

\paragraph{Data and code availability.}
The repository is public (Apache-2.0) with DCO sign-off. Datasets D2/D3/D4 and the recomputation
commands are described in \texttt{REPLICATION.md}. Every reported number is recomputed from landed
artifacts, and the paper marks any not-yet-landed value explicitly rather than filling in
estimates. Croissant and Responsible-AI metadata are \emph{registered TODOs} (to be generated before
submission once the 2027 specification is published), not yet shipped.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
All headline numbers are reproducible via single commands; the paper documents the exact
environment dependencies (WSL, \texttt{g++}, \texttt{setarch}) and reports \texttt{UNVERIFIED}
rather than a misleading low score when a dependency is missing.
