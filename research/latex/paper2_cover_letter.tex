To the Editors of \emph{Transactions on Machine Learning Research}, and (alternatively)
the NeurIPS 2027 Evaluations \& Datasets Chairs and Reviewers,

\bigskip
\noindent
We submit \textbf{``The Boundary of Evaluator Auditing: Identifiability, Correctability and
Sample Complexity of Measurement Drift''}, a theory paper on how far an audit of an evaluator can go.

\paragraph{This is the second of two papers, and it is deliberately complementary.}
Our first paper, submitted separately, is a \emph{case study}: it audits one C/C++ verification
apparatus and reports five findings about what that audit found. The present paper asks the
orthogonal question---what an audit can establish \emph{in principle}---and answers it with three
boundary results. We cite the first paper for its findings only, restate none of its arguments, and
reuse none of its headline numbers.

\paragraph{Four contributions.}
(1)~\textbf{A measurement-drift algebra}: seven axioms and six theorems (T1--T6). Its metatheory is
settled by exhaustive model checking over all $4096$ coverage structures crossed with seven
aggregation families---only \textbf{two} axioms are independent (monotonicity, super-additivity),
four are theorems, one is a meta-axiom---and the system is \textbf{incomplete}: label drift cannot
be expressed, so we register a missing \textbf{A8 (label-axis closure)} and extend the caliber
signature to a six-tuple. Caliber arbitrage is NP-hard with a greedy $(1-1/e)$ approximation.
(2)~\textbf{Three boundary results}: structural versus real drift is \emph{not identifiable} from
paired data alone but is point-identified under nested calibers; post-hoc repair is possible exactly
on the post-measurement layer, with residual error up to $30.0\%$ where it fails; and paired designs
have \emph{identically zero} power against label and aggregation drift.
(3)~\textbf{An information-theoretic audit bound}: component-removal drift costs $849$ paired samples
against $17$ for environment drift, so cost is governed by how many samples \emph{carry} the drift,
not by how large it looks; the paired step of our earlier protocol becomes a \emph{dual} design.
(4)~\textbf{Four validation experiments}, all computed from frozen artifacts of earlier batches, plus
a partial cross-domain arm on LLM-as-a-judge evaluation (migration score $55.84/100$).

\paragraph{What we do \emph{not} claim.}
No priority: the four-variant taxonomy of Goodhart's law and a two-dimensional construct-validity
profile for LLM judges are both already in the literature, and we cite them as nearest neighbours.
No new measurement: every number was computed in an earlier batch from a frozen matrix. No
universality: one C/C++ apparatus plus one partial LLM arm. And no completeness: the axiom system is
missing A8, and two of its axioms could not be separated within the tested domain.

\paragraph{Venue fit.}
The contribution is a set of boundaries rather than a system, and its central claims are negative.
That suits TMLR, where rigour is weighed over novelty narrative, and the E\&D track, whose remit
includes evaluation methodology and negative results.

\medskip
\noindent
We hope the boundaries prove useful to anyone who must decide whether an evaluation number can be
trusted, repaired, or must be re-measured.
